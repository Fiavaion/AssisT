AssisT Privacy Policy

Adaptive Accessibility Chrome Extension

Last Updated: September 26, 2026
Effective Date: April 12, 2026
Version: 1.0.0

Privacy at a glance

  • Nothing leaves your device until you say yes. AssisT asks about each online service separately, in setup or the first time a feature needs it, and remembers your answer
  • No data collection by us: we don't track you, and there is no Fiavaion server to send anything to
  • Local-only storage: your settings, notes, citations, and consent answers stay on your device
  • Every service named: the full list of services AssisT can reach, and exactly what each receives, is below — nothing outside that list is contacted
  • AI privacy options: local AI (Ollama) and in-browser AI (WebLLM) never send your text anywhere; cloud AI only sends it if you choose a provider and save your own key
  • Change your mind any time: every consent answer and saved key can be reviewed and revoked in the popup, under Advanced Options > Your data
  • Open source: our code is transparent and auditable on GitHub

1. Overview

AssisT is a Chrome browser extension that provides accessibility features for students and learners. This policy explains exactly what data AssisT accesses, stores, and, in the specific cases listed below, sends to an outside service — and what it never does.

Your educational data is yours. We don't need it and haven't built anything for AssisT to send it to us: there is no Fiavaion server that receives it.

2. What data we collect

We collect nothing. AssisT has no server of its own, so nothing you do in the extension reaches Fiavaion.

Usage analytics None collected
Personal information None collected
Browsing history None collected
Account or login Not required
Advertising IDs None collected

3. Consent: nothing leaves your device until you say yes

Every request AssisT makes to an outside service is checked against one rule: has the student said yes to that specific service. Until they have, the request is refused and nothing is sent.

3.1 How you are asked

The first-run setup asks about the services your own choices would use, one question per service, with Allow and Don't allow. If you skip setup, you are asked the same question the first time you use a feature that needs it. A skipped question is not a yes. Declining is never a dead end: a dictionary or translation you decline still explains itself rather than doing nothing.

3.2 How you change your mind

Every answer is listed under Advanced Options > Your data, in the AssisT popup. You can flip any answer at any time, and it takes effect on the very next request, not the next time you reload the page. The same screen lists and removes any AI keys you have saved.

3.3 What is never asked about

Nothing about how you use AssisT, or how it thinks you are getting on, is ever sent to a third party as part of that question. Your consent answers themselves stay on your device and are never included in a settings export.

4. Every service AssisT can reach, and what it receives

This is the complete list. If a service is not named here, AssisT does not contact it.

Free Dictionary API

  • Data sent: the word or short phrase you select for a lookup. If you press play on a pronunciation, the audio also loads from this service
  • When: required for the Dictionary feature; asked about once, gates every lookup after
  • Website: dictionaryapi.dev

MyMemory

  • Data sent: the text you ask to translate, and the two language codes. With Translate Page, all the visible text of the page (never hidden text or AssisT's own controls), which on a course page can include other people's names
  • When: the only translation provider AssisT uses; no API key needed. Translating a selection and Translate Page are asked about separately
  • Privacy policy: mymemory.translated.net

Crossref

  • Data sent: a DOI (a code like 10.1000/xyz123)
  • When: a citation already has a DOI, or you add a source by DOI. Crossref sends back the publication details AssisT fills the citation in with
  • Website: crossref.org

jsDelivr (text-recognition language files)

  • Data sent: only a request for one public language file. No image and no text from the page is sent
  • When: the first time Read from Images is used in a language other than English, after you allow it. English is built into AssisT and needs nothing. Each language is downloaded once and kept inside AssisT on your device
  • Website: jsdelivr.com

Semantic Scholar

  • Data sent: up to 120 characters of a citation's title
  • When: only if you turn on online citation checking, which is off by default and asks you twice — once for a Chrome permission to reach the site, and once for consent to send the title
  • Website: semanticscholar.org

Google speech recognition (dictation)

  • Data sent: the sound of your voice while dictation or the sticky-note microphone is listening, so that it can be turned into text
  • When: these use Chrome's own speech recognition, which is a Google service. AssisT asks before the first time either of them starts, and starts nothing without a yes

Google speech recognition (lecture capture)

  • Data sent: everything the microphone hears while lecture capture is on. That is the sound of the room, including other people's voices such as your lecturer and classmates, so that it can be turned into captions and notes
  • When: the lecture companion uses the same Google service as dictation, but it is asked about separately, because it hears more than you. A yes to dictation is not a yes to lecture capture. AssisT asks before the first time you start it, and starts nothing without a yes

Online read-aloud voices

  • Data sent: the text being read aloud, to Google (or Microsoft, for its online voices), which turns it into speech
  • When: only if you pick an online voice in the voice picker. Read-aloud uses a voice on your device by default, and the picker labels every online voice with who it sends the text to

4.1 Cloud AI providers

If you choose cloud AI and save your own API key for one of these, the text you ask an AI feature about is sent to that provider. Saving the key, or pressing Allow access for that provider in the popup, is how you say yes; removing the key under Your data withdraws consent immediately.

  • Anthropic (Claude)
  • OpenAI (ChatGPT)
  • Google Gemini
  • Perplexity

Each provider is subject to its own privacy policy once your request reaches it. AssisT is not an intermediary: your browser talks to the provider directly, and we never see or store what is sent.

4.2 Local AI: nothing leaves the device

  • Ollama runs on your own computer, reached over the loopback address (localhost). AssisT asks Chrome for permission to reach it, but no student text goes anywhere else
  • WebLLM (in-browser AI) downloads a model's weights once from Hugging Face, then runs entirely in your browser tab. The code that runs the model is built into AssisT and is never downloaded. After the download, no student text leaves the device
Important: Fiavaion does not receive, store, or have access to anything sent to the services above. When you use cloud AI, translation, or citation lookups, the request goes directly from your browser to that service — we are not in the middle of it.

5. What is stored, and where

Everything AssisT stores lives in Chrome's own storage on your device (chrome.storage.local, and, for citations and annotations, IndexedDB). None of it syncs to a Fiavaion server, because there isn't one.

5.1 Settings and preferences

  • Feature toggles, and which features are on or off
  • Text-to-Speech settings (voice, speed, pitch, highlight colours)
  • Speech-to-Text settings (language, punctuation mode)
  • Visual settings (font, spacing, dark mode, overlay colours)
  • Keyboard shortcut customisations
  • Your chosen profile (ADHD, Dyslexia, and so on)
  • Translation language preferences
  • AI mode and model preferences

5.2 Content you create

  • Annotations and sticky notes: stored locally, organised by the page you created them on
  • Citations: bibliography entries and citation projects, stored locally
  • Custom vocabulary: words you add to your Speech-to-Text dictionary

5.3 Consent answers

Your yes or no to each service listed in Section 4, stored locally, and excluded from settings exports on purpose.

5.4 Saved AI keys

If you save a key for a cloud AI provider, it is encrypted with a key derived from a random identifier generated on your device and stored alongside it. This is worth being plain about: it protects a saved key from casual inspection, such as someone glancing at storage in DevTools or opening a settings export. It does not protect the key from someone who can read the extension's own storage on that computer, because everything needed to decrypt it sits in that same storage. If you use AssisT on a shared or public computer, remove your keys under Your data when you are finished.

5.5 Settings export

Exporting your settings, to move to a new computer or keep as a backup, never includes an AI key or a consent answer. Only preferences travel; the export is built from a fixed list of setting names, not a copy of everything in storage, so a credential can't be added to it by accident.

6. Chrome permissions explained

AssisT requests the following Chrome permissions. Here is exactly why each is needed:

storage
Save your preferences and settings locally on your device
activeTab
Apply accessibility features to the page you're currently viewing
tabs
Detect when you navigate to supported educational sites
contextMenus
Add right-click options for quick access to features
scripting
Inject accessibility features (TTS controls, reading guides, etc.) into web pages
offscreen
Run in-browser AI (WebLLM) and other on-device work that needs a page context the background script does not have

6.1 Default website access

By default, AssisT only runs on these educational platforms:

  • *.instructure.com and *.canvas.com (Canvas LMS)
  • *.moodle.org and *.moodlecloud.com (Moodle)
  • classroom.google.com (Google Classroom)
  • docs.google.com (Google Docs)

On these sites, AssisT can read page content to provide accessibility features. This content is processed locally and is never sent to an outside service except the ones named in Section 4, and only once you have said yes to that one.

6.2 Optional: enable everywhere

You may choose to grant AssisT permission to run on all websites. This is:

  • Completely optional — AssisT works fully on educational sites without it
  • Requested only when you choose "Enable Everywhere" — never automatically
  • Revocable at any time in Chrome Settings → Extensions → AssisT → Site access

The services in Section 4 that need their own host permission are requested the same way: none is requested at install. Each one (Dictionary, Translation, Crossref, jsDelivr, Semantic Scholar, Ollama's loopback address, and the cloud AI providers in Section 4.1) is requested only when you allow that service or save a key, in the same step as the consent question. Withdrawing your consent under Your data gives the permission back.

7. What AssisT never does

  • Collect or store personal information
  • Track your browsing activity
  • Use analytics or tracking services
  • Share data with advertisers
  • Sell or monetise user data
  • Require account creation or login
  • Transmit data to a Fiavaion server (we have none that receives user data)
  • Contact a service not named in Section 4

8. Students and children

AssisT is built for students, including students under 13. We do not collect personal information from anyone, there is no account to create, and there is no data gathered that would call for a parent's consent in the first place.

If your school deploys AssisT for its students, we are glad to talk through exactly what is described in this policy with whoever there needs to sign off on it.

9. Information for institutions and data protection officers

We are not going to describe AssisT as certified compliant with FERPA, COPPA, or GDPR. What we can tell you, precisely, is what data moves, when, and to whom: that is Section 4 above, in full. If your institution needs that read against a specific legal requirement, your own data protection officer is better placed to make that judgement than we are, and this policy is written to give them what they need to do it:

  • No student education record is ever collected or stored by Fiavaion, because there is no Fiavaion server to store it on
  • Everything is processed in the student's own browser, on their own device
  • Data reaches a third party only for the specific, named reasons in Section 4, and only after the student has consented to that one service
  • A school can deploy AssisT without a data-sharing agreement with Fiavaion, because there is no data flow to Fiavaion to cover

9.1 European users

Because AssisT has no server receiving personal data, most of the obligations GDPR places on a data controller do not arise here. What we can offer instead is full local control:

  • Access: view everything stored via the extension's own screens, or Chrome DevTools (Application → Storage)
  • Rectification: edit any stored preference or content through the extension
  • Erasure: delete data through the extension's own controls, or remove it all by uninstalling
  • Portability: export your settings, annotations, and citations as files
  • Objection: disable any feature, or uninstall, at any time

If you believe your data protection rights have been affected, contact us first at info@fiavaion.com. EU/EEA residents may also contact their local Data Protection Authority; UK residents may contact the Information Commissioner's Office.

10. Data security

  • HTTPS only: every request to a service in Section 4 uses a secure HTTPS connection
  • Saved AI keys are encrypted at rest, as described in Section 5.4
  • No server to breach: because there is no Fiavaion server holding student data, there is no Fiavaion database of it to be exposed
  • Content sanitisation: AssisT uses DOMPurify to sanitise HTML content and prevent cross-site scripting

11. Your choices

  • Review or change any consent answer: Advanced Options > Your data, in the popup
  • Remove a saved AI key: same screen
  • Disable a feature: toggle it off in the popup at any time
  • Delete annotations or citations: remove them from their own panels
  • Revoke site access: change permissions in Chrome Settings → Extensions
  • Uninstall: removes everything Chrome stored for the extension

12. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the extension changes. Changes will be:
