Pre-talk Setup Checklist

Slides

0:00 – 5:30
Slide 01 Title [0:00] ~15 sec
"Good [morning/afternoon] everyone. I'm Mark Jones — Digital Technical Officer at NCAD — and I've spent the last two years building something I want to show you today. It's called AssisT."
Slide 02 Who I Am [0:15] ~45 sec
"Quick context. Eighteen years at NCAD. Two years ago I moved into assistive technology work, and immediately ran into the problem that I suspect most of you deal with every week — the tools we have are expensive, fragmented, and locked to a specific machine or institution. Students build four years of habits around a tool. They graduate. It disappears.

That gap is what started AssisT."
▶ PAUSE — let the quote on the slide land before advancing
Slide 03 The Problem [1:00] ~60 sec
"Four problems on this slide — and I'd bet you recognise every one of them.

Cost and gatekeeping. TTS tools locked to lab machines, or requiring a formal diagnosis before a student can even try them.

Empty platforms. Canvas, Moodle, Google Classroom ship with essentially zero built-in reading or writing support. For a student with dyslexia, that's not a small gap — that's the whole thing.

The discovery gap. First-generation students, EAL learners, students who don't self-identify as needing AT — they often don't know what's available, and nobody tells them.

The support cliff. Institutional licence ends at graduation. The tool a student built four years of habits around disappears exactly when they're entering a workplace and need it most."
Slide 04 What is AssisT [2:00] ~30 sec
"AssisT is a Chrome extension. Installs in under a minute from the Chrome Web Store. Works on Canvas, Moodle, Google Classroom — and every other site.

No account required. Nothing leaves the browser. GDPR compliant. Free and open source.

That last point matters — it means the support cliff doesn't exist. The tool doesn't disappear when funding ends."
Slide 05 Reading Help [2:30] ~20 sec
Slides 05–09 are intentionally quick — name the category and move on. Save the detail for the demo.
"I'll move through the next six feature slides quickly because I'd rather show you than tell you.

Reading: TTS with word-by-word highlight, a reading mode that strips the noise from any page, dyslexia mode with bionic reading, and OCR for scanned PDFs and images."
Slide 06 Writing Help [2:50] ~20 sec
"Writing: speech-to-text that works in any text field on any website — Canvas assignment submissions, Moodle forum posts, Google Docs — using the browser's built-in engine. Nothing leaves the device."
Slide 07 Visual Comfort [3:10] ~20 sec
"Visual comfort: colour overlay for Meares-Irlen-type visual stress, a focus mode with a clear reading window for attention difficulties, a tracking guide line, and global motion suppression for vestibular sensitivities."
Slide 08 Study Tools [3:30] ~20 sec
"Study tools: annotations, sticky notes, RSVP speed reading, a Pomodoro timer, accessibility profiles — I'll show those shortly — and full WCAG-compliant text customisation: font, size, line spacing, letter and word spacing."
Slide 09 LMS Integration [3:50] ~15 sec
"Purpose-built for Canvas, Moodle, and Google Classroom. And crucially — it detects the LMS automatically. Students don't configure anything."
Slide 10 AI Assist [4:05] ~25 sec
"Eight AI-powered features — summarisation, text simplification, assignment breakdown, a Socratic tutor, knowledge graphs, study path generator, citation analyser, multi-doc compare.

Privacy-first by default — runs locally on the student's device. No data goes to AssisT servers. If a student wants cloud AI, they supply their own API key. We never touch it."
Slide 11 Free for Everyone [4:30] ~30 sec
Emotional peak for this audience — "Nothing goes through IT procurement" will get a reaction. Let it land.
"No subscription. No waitlist. No institutional licence required.

Any student installs it in sixty seconds. You can recommend it today and they can have it running before they leave this session. A lecturer can model it in class. A DSO can add it to their handout. Nothing goes through IT procurement."
Slide 12 How You Can Help [5:00] ~15 sec
"Four steps for after this session — install it, share it, watch the tutorial series on YouTube, give feedback on GitHub. Links are at the bottom — I'll leave this up.

Let me show you what it actually does."
→ Switch to browser — do NOT advance slides yet. Return to slide 14 for Q&A.
Slide 13 Roadmap
"Three things coming that are directly relevant to your work.

Firefox and Safari support — so AssisT reaches students on every platform.

An STT voice profile creator — so students with Dysarthria can train the recognition engine to their speech, instead of adapting to the engine.

And Exam Mode — a password-locked profile configured by the AT technician. Only the supports on the student's accommodation plan stay active. Everything else — AI, summarisation, web access — is locked. No more all-or-nothing workarounds at exam time."
Plant the Exam Mode seed — it will come up in Q&A regardless.

You're live in the browser. Speak to the screen — point at what you're clicking so remote attendees can follow.
Demo 1 Popup Overview [5:30] ~45 sec
Click the AssisT icon in the Chrome toolbar.
"This is the control centre. Everything is organised into sections — Reading, Writing, Visual, Study Tools, AI. By default nothing is on. Students turn on what they need — and it remembers.

Let me start with the feature most of your students will reach for first."
Demo 2 TTS with Word Highlighting [6:15] ~2:00
This is the moment to let breathe. Don't rush — word-level sync on a webpage is often new to this audience.
Switch to Tab 1. Select a paragraph. Press TTS play.
"Text to speech. I'll select a paragraph and play."
Let it run 3–4 sentences. Watch the room.
"Watch the highlight — word by word, exactly in sync with the voice.

Now — the interaction that students tell me makes the biggest difference:"
Click somewhere in a different paragraph.
"Click anywhere on the page and the reader jumps to that sentence. They stop being a passenger in their own reading. They can navigate by clicking."
Open TTS settings. Move the speed slider.
"Speed: 0.5x for students who need processing time, up to 2x for students who just want a faster scan. Set once, remembered. Voice choice is here too — any voice the browser has installed."
Demo 3 Reading Mode [8:15] ~45 sec
Toggle Reading Mode from the popup.
"Reading mode. One click."
▶ Pause — let the page transform register with the audience
"Navigation: gone. Ads: gone. Sidebars: gone. Font, size, and spacing snap to whatever the student has saved. For a student with ADHD trying to read a dense university page — this alone can be the difference between reading it and not."
Demo 4 Visual Comfort [9:00] ~60 sec
Open Visual section. Toggle colour overlay.
"Colour overlay."
Adjust colour and intensity.
"Colour, intensity, blend mode — all adjustable. Evidence-based for Meares-Irlen-type visual stress. Students who've relied on a physical tinted overlay for print — they now have it on screen, on every website they use."
Toggle off overlay. Toggle Focus Mode.
"Focus mode. Dark overlay, clear reading window that follows the cursor."
Move mouse slowly across the page.
"For students with attention difficulties — keeps eyes on one area at a time. One-second toggle."
Demo 5 Speech to Text [10:00] ~90 sec
If Chrome asks for mic permission — grant it. On file:// pages Chrome doesn't remember mic permission by design. Say so out loud: "On a real site like Canvas this only happens once." This is NOT an AssisT bug.
Switch to Tab 3 (text field). Click the floating mic icon.
"Speech to text. I'll click the mic."
Dictate a sentence or two clearly.
"Direct into the text field — no copy-paste, no intermediate window. Works in Canvas submissions, Moodle forum posts, Google Docs, email, search boxes — anywhere there's a text box on any website.

Browser's own speech engine — nothing leaves the device.

For a student with motor difficulties, or dysgraphia, or who simply composes better verbally than in writing — this removes the transcription barrier. They think, they speak, it's there."
Demo 6 AI: Text Simplification [11:30] ~90 sec
Switch to Tab 2. Select a complex paragraph.
"AI features. I'll select some dense academic text."
Trigger text simplification. Wait for response.
"The same content, rewritten at an accessible reading level.

By default this runs locally — Ollama or the browser's built-in AI. Nothing leaves the student's machine. If they have their own Claude or Gemini API key they can use cloud AI — but AssisT never sees the data, never logs it, never sends it anywhere.

Assignment breakdown is equally useful — paste in a brief, get back a structured checklist. For a student who struggles to extract actionable steps from a wall of text — that's a significant cognitive load reduction."
Demo 7 Accessibility Profiles [13:00] ~45 sec
Open popup → Profiles.
"Accessibility profiles. A profile is a complete saved state — TTS voice and speed, visual settings, text spacing, everything — in one name.

A student can have a 'Focus session' profile and a 'Reading aloud' profile and switch in one tap.

Crucially:"
Point to export/import.
"Export and import as JSON. If you — as an AT technician — set up the optimal configuration for a student, you export it and they import it. They don't need to sit with you going through every single setting. You build it once, they get it instantly."
Demo 8 Wrap [13:45] ~30 sec
"That's maybe a third of what's in here — I haven't shown you annotations, sticky notes, the knowledge graph, RSVP reading, the Pomodoro timer. The full feature guide is on the Fiavaion website — I'll drop the link in the chat.

Everything you've just seen: free, installs in sixty seconds, works on any site."

Q&A

Q&A

15:00 – 20:00
Slide 14 on screen with contact details. "I'll leave the contact details up. What questions do you have?"
"What if a student doesn't have Chrome?"
Works on any Chromium browser — Edge, Brave, Opera. Firefox and Safari are on the roadmap.
"Can I configure it for a student before they come to see me?"
Yes. Build the profile you want, export it as JSON, send it or import it when they sit down. Takes about five minutes to set up once.
"What about exams?"
Exam Mode is the next major feature — password-locked profile configured by you. Only the supports on the accommodation plan stay active; AI and web access locked. Not in current release, but it's the top priority.
"Is this GDPR compliant?"
Yes. Nothing leaves the browser by default. Settings stored locally. Nothing sent to Fiavaion servers. Open source — anyone can audit the code.
"Is it just for students?"
No — any person, any website. Lecturers use it for their own reading. Graduates keep it after they leave.
"Will it stay free?"
Yes. EUPL open-source licence — it can't be made proprietary. We're pursuing grant funding to sustain development long-term.
"How do students find out it exists?"
That's partly why I'm here. The biggest barrier right now is discovery. If you add it to your AT resources and DSO handouts, that's the most valuable thing that can happen after this session.
