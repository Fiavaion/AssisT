Critical Cultures: Visual Analysis Essay

VICU1001 15 Credits Due: Week 12

Assignment Overview

This essay asks you to engage critically with visual culture through close analysis of a single artwork, design object, or cultural artifact. Drawing on theories and methodologies introduced in lectures and seminars, you will develop an argument about how your chosen object produces meaning within its social, historical, and cultural contexts.

Visual culture studies examines not just what images represent, but how they function within broader systems of power, identity, and communication. Your essay should demonstrate your ability to "read" visual objects critically, moving beyond surface description to analyse underlying structures of meaning.

Learning Outcomes

On successful completion of this assignment, you will be able to:

  • Observation: Demonstrate close observation skills in describing visual objects with precision and attention to detail
  • Research: Locate, evaluate, and synthesise relevant secondary sources using appropriate academic databases
  • Analysis: Apply theoretical frameworks from visual culture studies to interpret visual material
  • Argument: Construct a coherent written argument supported by evidence from primary and secondary sources
  • Professional Practice: Present work according to academic conventions including Harvard referencing

Essay Requirements

Word Count 1,500-2,000 words
Referencing Harvard Style
Minimum Sources 5 academic sources
Submission Canvas Assignment

Suggested Theoretical Approaches

You may find the following theoretical frameworks useful for your analysis. This is not an exhaustive list, and you are encouraged to explore approaches relevant to your chosen object:

  • Semiotics: How does your object communicate through signs and symbols? Consider Barthes' concepts of denotation and connotation, or Peirce's icon/index/symbol classification.
  • The Gaze: How does your object position the viewer? Consider Mulvey's theory of the male gaze, or Foucault's analysis of surveillance and power.
  • Representation: How does your object represent identity categories such as gender, race, class, or nationality? Consider Hall's encoding/decoding model.
  • Material Culture: How do the physical properties of your object shape its meanings? Consider the relationship between form, function, and cultural significance.

Assessment Criteria

Criterion Weighting Description
Visual Analysis 30% Detailed and insightful description of the visual object demonstrating close observation
Theoretical Application 30% Effective application of relevant theoretical frameworks to interpret the object
Research & Sources 20% Evidence of independent research using appropriate academic sources
Argument & Structure 10% Clear thesis statement, logical progression, and coherent conclusion
Academic Writing 10% Clear expression, correct Harvard referencing, and professional presentation

Harvard Referencing Example

In-text citation:

As Berger (1972, p.7) argues, "Seeing comes before words. The child looks and recognizes before it can speak."

Reference list entry:

Berger, J. (1972) Ways of Seeing. London: British Broadcasting Corporation and Penguin Books.

Recommended Reading

  • Berger, J. (1972) Ways of Seeing. London: BBC/Penguin.
  • Hall, S. (ed.) (1997) Representation: Cultural Representations and Signifying Practices. London: Sage.
  • Mirzoeff, N. (2015) How to See the World. London: Pelican.
  • Rose, G. (2016) Visual Methodologies: An Introduction to Researching with Visual Materials. 4th ed. London: Sage.
  • Sturken, M. and Cartwright, L. (2017) Practices of Looking: An Introduction to Visual Culture. 3rd ed. Oxford: Oxford University Press.
